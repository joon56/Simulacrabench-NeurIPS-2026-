\begin{abstract}
  Held-out respondents are completed from their GIVEN block by a calibrated base predictor and additions that are admitted only by the validation run inside the submission. The base predictor uses hierarchical backoff tables, a shared-trunk network with a softmax head per item, soft coupling to the declared skip logic, and donor evidence (the answers of the most similar visible respondents under Fellegi-Sunter agreement weights, sharpened on UNHCR by section-specific metrics learned from the visible rows). On top of the base predictor, each instrument may receive one transform chain built from one or more of these parts: a log-linear pool with gradient-boosted tree experts or a pretrained in-context tabular model (TabICL v2), a copy share for exact matches, and a reliability calibration. The chain is fitted on 1,000 respondents hidden inside the run and applied only if 1,000 further hidden respondents confirm it. The test-phase skill is 0.479 (UNICEF 0.343, World Bank 0.234, UNHCR 0.860). In development, 24 variants never read above the base by one display step, so we claim nothing about the size of the additions' contribution.
\end{abstract}

\subsection{Pipeline}
\label{sec:pipeline}
The grader calls \texttt{predict} once per instrument with the complete visible respondents
and the held-out respondents, of whom only the GIVEN block is shown. Everything is fitted
inside that call; the archive holds no parameter estimated from survey responses.
(1)~\emph{Base.} A predictor (Section~1.4) is fitted on all visible rows and yields one
probability vector per held-out cell; on World Bank and UNICEF it is the cell-wise mean over
three network seeds. (2)~\emph{Guard.} A fixed permutation splits the visible rows into
$S$ (1,000), $E$ (1,000) and $F$ (rest). The answers of $S$ and $E$ are hidden, and a shadow
base and the experts are fitted on $F$ alone, so their predictions for $S$ and $E$ are
honest. Candidate chains (Section~1.6) are fitted on $S$ and ranked by a two-fold
out-of-fold score within $S$. The best two are tested on $E$ in order; a chain is accepted
if $\bar d - 1.0\,\mathrm{sd}(d)/\sqrt{n} > 0$, where $d$ is the per-respondent difference
in mean floored log probability to the shadow base. (3)~\emph{Application.} An accepted
chain is refitted on $S \cup E$ and applied to the base output of step~1, with its experts
refitted on all visible rows. Otherwise the instrument keeps the base output.
The base follows record-linkage practice \citep{fellegi1969} and retrieval-augmented tabular
learning \citep{gorishniy2024tabr}; the guard is a small cross-validated stacking procedure
\citep{wolpert1992,vanderlaan2007}.

\subsection{Inputs and feature construction}
Used: the GIVEN columns as categorical levels, the answers of visible respondents, and the
schema (item classes, option lists, gate parents and opening values). Count-like levels
(``3'', ``6--10'', ``11+'') are also parsed into numbers and summed into household totals
for the tree experts. Not used: question text and item descriptions, survey weights (not
released), respondent identifiers and row order (the organizers state they carry no
information), external data. Gated items get the schema's gate value as an extra final
option, predicted like any other answer.

\subsection{Model or simulator}
\textbf{Base predictor.}
(i)~\emph{Backoff tables}: per item, a ladder of conditional tables over up to three GIVEN
columns (cardinality $\le 12$), each level shrunk toward the coarser one with pseudo-count
$\kappa \in \{1,3,10,30,100\}$.
(ii)~\emph{Network}: one-hot GIVEN features, a two-layer trunk (64 or 128 units), one softmax
head per item including the gate value and one asked-only head per gated item; one
weight per instrument mixes network and tables.
(iii)~\emph{Soft gates}: the gate-implied distribution, computed from the predicted parent
distribution, is mixed with the unrestricted head using a shrunken agreement rate between
the declared gate and the visible rows; a gate the data contradict is not enforced.
(iv)~\emph{Donor evidence}: for every held-out row the visible rows are scored by weighted
agreement on the GIVEN columns, with Fellegi-Sunter weights $\log(m_j/u_j) -
\log((1-m_j)/(1-u_j))$ estimated from provisional best-match pairs \citep{fellegi1969};
the softmax-weighted answer shares of the ten best donors are mixed into the model
distribution with a weight $\lambda \in [0, 0.98]$ per item and per match-concentration
bucket, chosen by a self-excluded replay on visible rows.
(v)~\emph{Supervised section metrics (UNHCR only)}: per questionnaire section, a diagonal
metric over GIVEN-agreement indicators re-weights 32 candidate donors; it maximizes the
floored log probability of the donors' answers on 1,200 visible query rows and replaces
(iv) only where it gains on 800 separate rows by more than $\max(0.003, 2\,\mathrm{se})$.

\textbf{Experts} (used only inside chains).
$g$: gradient-boosted trees \citep{ke2017lightgbm,pedregosa2011} per item on the GIVEN
columns and household totals.
$r$: the same plus the answers, match scores and soft votes of the nearest visible rows and,
per item, the donor answer shares under the metrics of (iv) and (v) with the three heaviest
donors' answers; training rows get leave-one-out neighbours.
$q$: $r$ plus four donor-only metric fields that mark donors whose own best answer-agreement
with another visible row is unusually high (a robust $z$-score), i.e.\ donors that already
have a twin among the visible rows.
TabICL: a pretrained in-context tabular classifier \citep{qu2025tabicl} fitted per item on
the GIVEN columns, without gradient updates.
UNHCR uses $r$ and $q$; World Bank and UNICEF use $g$ and TabICL.

\subsection{Fitting, training, or prompting}
All fitting uses the visible rows of the phase (test phase: TRAIN and DEV). The network
minimizes pooled cross-entropy with Adam (learning rate $3\cdot10^{-3}$, weight decay
$10^{-4}$, at most 500 epochs, patience 30); width, dropout, the table ladder and the
mixture weights are selected on an internal 20\% split and the network is refitted on all
visible rows. Trees: 120 iterations at most, learning rate 0.1, 15 leaves, early stopping.
Section metrics: L-BFGS-B, 50 iterations, weights in $[0,12]$. Nothing was tuned on test
data, and no test-phase feedback existed before the single upload.

\subsection{Prediction and aggregation}
Chains offered to the guard are sequences of the following parts.
\emph{Pool}: $p \propto p_{\mathrm{base}}^{a}\prod_k p_k^{b_k}$ with exponents in $[0,3]$
maximizing the log-likelihood on $S$, starting at the base itself $(1,0,\dots)$
\citep{genest1986}; a per-item variant shrinks item exponents to the global ones.
\emph{Copy share}: $(1-\rho)\,p + \rho\,e$, where $e$ is the answer share among visible rows
tied at the best match score and $\rho = 0.9995\,\sigma(a + x^\top g)$ depends on label-free
match-quality features.
\emph{Calibration}: Section~1.7.
UNHCR is offered 15 chains (pools with $r$, $q$ or both, with and without copy share and
calibration); World Bank and UNICEF 8 chains (pools with $g$, TabICL or both, with and
without calibration). At most one chain per instrument is applied. The guard's decision is
made on the organizers' machine and is not observable to us; no statistic of the data is
encoded in the outputs.

\subsection{Post-processing and calibration (if applicable)}
The calibration part bins the top-class probability above 0.5 (edges 0.7, 0.8, 0.9, 0.95,
0.98, 0.99, 0.995, 0.999), replaces it by the smoothed, monotone observed accuracy on $S$
(50 pseudo-cells toward the stated confidence, cap $1-10^{-4}$) and rescales the other
classes; it is applied only if the guard accepts a chain containing it. No clipping is
applied beyond the grader's own floor of $10^{-3}$.

\subsection{Breakdown by population, survey, or question type}
The leaderboard exposes one skill per instrument. The method does worst on World Bank
(0.234): its nine GIVEN columns carry little about test scores and post-assessment answers,
and it has no donor structure: neighbour-based experts read two to three development
display steps below the base there. It does best on UNHCR (0.860), where the answers of
similar visible households are the main source of skill: in development, the base without
donor evidence read 0.79 on the former uniform-reference scale and 0.92 with it.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

The method predicts marginal distributions per cell and says nothing about the joint
distribution of a respondent's answers. Its skill on UNHCR rests on visible respondents who
closely resemble the held-out one; for a population without such neighbours it falls back
to the base tables and network, which read far lower. We cannot attribute the test score to
components: the guard's accepted chains are unobservable, the development board resolved
only differences of about 0.01 on the former scale, and all 24 development uploads of
variants read in the base's bins.\label{endmain}

\section{Full configuration}
\textbf{Base (file \texttt{e041\_core.py}).} Tables: columns with cardinality $\le 12$, depth
$\le 3$, $\kappa$ grid $\{1,3,10,30,100\}$, selection on a 20\% split (seed 0, at most 3,000
evaluation rows). Network: configurations (64 units, no dropout) and (128 units, dropout
0.1), two layers, Adam, learning rate $3\cdot10^{-3}$, weight decay $10^{-4}$, at most 500
epochs, patience 30; network/table weight grid $\{0, 0.1, \dots, 1\}$. Gate agreement:
shrinkage pseudo-count 20, optionally conditioned on one GIVEN column (cardinality $\le 12$).
Donor evidence: all GIVEN columns as signature, rarity weights $-\log\sum_v f_v^2$ for a
first pass, provisional pairs at top-1 posterior mass $\ge 0.5$ (at least 30 pairs,
otherwise rarity weights), ten donors, $\lambda$ grid $\{0, 0.1, \dots, 0.9, 0.95, 0.98\}$,
two buckets split at maximum posterior weight 0.5 (at least 30 rows per bucket), at most
6,000 replay queries. Section metrics (UNHCR): sections from the item-name prefix, 32
candidates, 1,200 fit / 800 selection / 800 replay queries, L-BFGS-B with 50 iterations,
weights in $[0,12]$, penalty $10^{-3}$ on the squared deviation from the initial weights,
activation if the gain exceeds $\max(0.003, 2\,\mathrm{se})$. World Bank and UNICEF use the
base without section metrics.

\textbf{Seeds.} Network seeds $\{0,1,2\}$ averaged cell-wise on World Bank and UNICEF, seed 0
on UNHCR. Averaging probabilities cannot lower the expected log score of a single run
(Jensen; the grader's floor is affine).

\textbf{Guard (file \texttt{main.py}).} Permutation PCG64(4501) of the visible rows; $|S| =
|E| = 1{,}000$; shadow base with seed 0; ranking by the pooled floored log score, two-fold
out-of-fold within $S$ for chains with fitted parts; at most two chains tested on $E$;
one-sided quantile 1.0; refit on $S \cup E$ after acceptance.

\textbf{Tree experts.} \texttt{HistGradientBoostingClassifier}: learning rate 0.1, at most 120
iterations, 15 leaves, minimum 10 samples per leaf, L2 1.0, 255 bins, early stopping
(validation fraction 0.15, patience 6), seed 0; output mixed with 0.002 uniform mass.
Neighbour features: three nearest visible rows by the agreement weights (answer, score,
agreement flags) and soft votes of the ten nearest. Retrieval features per item: donor shares
under the learned and the initial metric, the three largest donor weights and those donors'
answers. Pairing fields of $q$: robust $z$ (median and MAD over the 32 nearest visible rows)
of a visible row's best answer agreement, thresholds 2.5, 3.5 and 5 (``no twin'' fields) and
$\ge 5$ (``has a twin''); for a training row the donor's score excludes that row.

\textbf{TabICL.} Eight ensemble members, normalization methods none and power, softmax
temperature 0.9, logits averaged, seed 42, mixed precision on GPU.

\textbf{Pool.} Log-probabilities clipped at $10^{-6}$; exponents in $[0,3]$, L-BFGS-B, 60
iterations; per-item variant with ridge 0.02 toward the global exponents.
\textbf{Copy share.} Five candidates searched, ties at the best score; features: per-field
disagreement with the best row, exact-match multiplicity ($\ge 2$, $\ge 3$), agreement of
the two best rows' answers, tie indicator; ridge 0.002 (features) and 0.005 (per-item
shifts); two starting points, the better optimum is kept.

\textbf{Chains.} UNHCR: \texttt{xlpool, xlpoolh, xlpool+cal, xlpoolh+cal, qlpool, qlpoolh,
qlpoolh+cal, rlpool, rlpoolh, rlpoolh+cal, xlpoolh+twinh, xlpoolh+twinh+cal, twinh,
twinh+cal, cal} (\texttt{x}: both experts; \texttt{h}: per-item parameters). World Bank and
UNICEF: \texttt{xlpool, xlpoolh, xlpool+cal, xlpoolh+cal, lpool, lpoolh, glpool, cal}
(\texttt{lpool}: TabICL alone; \texttt{glpool}: $g$ alone).
